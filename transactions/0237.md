# BRC-237: Payment Evidence Statements for BREAD and BEEF

RexStarBSV (RexStarBSV@protonmail.com)

## Abstract

This BRC defines a payment evidence statement, a small canonical JSON object in which a receiver records what it verified about an unmined payment and what it did not: the ancestors anchored by a Merkle path to a header it trusts, whether every input reaches one, the longest and shortest unconfirmed distance to an anchor, which unconfirmed transactions passed script, amount and lock-time checks, and the payment's observed status. A receiver that reports ancestry distance, as BREAD does, and a receiver that holds a BEEF can each make one from the evidence it already has. No statement says that a payment will be mined. The BRC also converts Electrum-form Merkle branches to [BRC-74](./0074.md) paths and defines an optional signature and wallet method.

## Motivation

Two kinds of evidence are in use for a payment that is not yet mined.

The first is ancestry distance. The receiver walks back from the payment through its unconfirmed ancestors until it reaches a transaction with a Merkle path to a block header, and reports the number of steps. The number records distance; which checks were run is a separate fact. ElectrumSVP's release notes for version 0.2.0-beta.1 describe evidence of this kind under the name BREAD: "Improved BREAD verification, including local proof-data handling, unconfirmed transaction verification, confirmed-ancestor anchoring, hop reporting, and BREAD JSON export". Its 0.2.0 release notes expand the name as Blockchain Referenced Evidence and Data.

The second is BEEF ([BRC-62](./0062.md), with the Atomic form of [BRC-95](./0095.md) and the V2 form of [BRC-96](./0096.md)), which carries unconfirmed ancestors back to transactions with [BRC-74](./0074.md) Merkle paths. With it, a receiver can run every script, check every signature, and check that each transaction's inputs cover its outputs, the checks that [BRC-67](./0067.md) lists for simplified payment verification. When it does, the result answers whether every spend from those coins to the payment is valid. The BEEF alone does not say whether a receiver ran those checks, or against which headers.

Neither kind of evidence answers whether the payment will be mined, or whether a conflicting spend of one of its inputs exists. Those depend on what miners do next. A receiver can look for a conflicting spend at a given moment, through a node it runs or a service it asks, and that observation is the most it can hold.

Each kind of evidence is described in its own terms today. "Three hops" and "a valid BEEF" may describe the same payment, and neither phrase alone lists the checks behind it. A statement in common terms lets:

- a user see what their wallet checked before they hand over goods;
- an application apply one acceptance policy, whichever wallet serves it;
- a wallet of either kind report what it knows, without adopting the other kind's format or architecture.

## Scope and conformance

**Layer.** Interface: what one program tells another about a payment, whether wallet to application, wallet to user or wallet to wallet. Whether to accept a payment is policy, and this document does not set it.

Three roles are used, and a MUST binds only an implementation that claims the role its sentence names:

- a **maker** produces statements (sections 2 to 4, 6 and 7);
- a **reader** reads them (section 5);
- a **wallet that offers the method** of section 8, which that section binds.

Adoption is voluntary. A wallet that makes no statements does not follow this document, and its other behaviour is unaffected.

**What this document does not require.** How a maker obtains its material or its block headers; how it stores anything; which transport carries a statement; whether a maker validates at all (a statement whose validation is "none" conforms); whether and how it checks for a conflicting spend; the values of its bounds and of a reader's; and the [BRC-100](../wallet/0100.md) interface, which section 8 uses optionally. A statement is self-contained: reading it needs no service and no key, and checking its anchors needs only block headers.

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174 when, and only when, they appear in capitals.

## Specification

### 1. Terms

- **Payment**: the transaction a statement is about.
- **Txid**: the double SHA-256 of a transaction's bytes. Inside a transaction, a BEEF, an Atomic BEEF's prefix and a BRC-74 path, a txid is in internal byte order, the order the hash gives; in a statement, and in this document's tables, it is 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits in display order, the reverse. A maker MUST compute every txid from the bytes it holds. An input names the transaction it spends by txid in internal byte order, and the maker matches it against the txids it computed in that same order. A txid written beside the bytes is a claim.
- **Material**: what the maker holds of the payment's ancestry, of one of two kinds: a BEEF as received ([BRC-62](./0062.md) V1, `0100beef`, or [BRC-96](./0096.md) V2, `0200beef`, alone or inside a [BRC-95](./0095.md) Atomic BEEF), or raw transactions and Merkle paths from the maker's own records. A statement is made over one material.
- **Header source**: the block headers the maker trusts, by height, and its tip, the highest of them; the source holds the tip's header. How the maker obtains them is outside this document. Every anchor is only as good as this source (see Security Considerations).
- **Median time past**: the median of the timestamps (bytes 68 to 71 of the 80-byte header, little-endian) of the 11 blocks ending at the header source's tip, that is the tip and the 10 blocks below it. A maker whose header source lacks one of those headers has no median time past.
- **Merkle path**: BRC-74 bytes. In a BEEF, a path counts for a transaction only when that transaction's entry names its index: BRC-62's has-BUMP byte `01` and index, or BRC-96's format `01` and index. In records, a path is held for a txid, as BRC-74 bytes or as an Electrum-form branch (section 6).
- **Own path**: for a txid at offset `x` of level 0 of a path of tree height `h` (the lowest such offset, if the txid is at more than one), the `h` steps that fold it. At step `i`, from 0 to `h - 1`, the working hash and its sibling, the node at offset `(x >> i) XOR 1` of level `i`, are hashed together, the left node first, as the double SHA-256 of their 64 bytes. The sibling is the hash the path gives at that offset, the working hash again where the path marks that offset a duplicate (flag `01`), or the node computed from the two nodes below it. The working hash starts as the txid and its last value is the root. A step with no sibling ends the fold with no root, and a txid that is not at level 0 has no own path. A one-transaction block is the exception: a path of tree height 1 that holds one node, the txid at offset 0, has no step, and its root is the txid.
- **Anchor**: a transaction the maker holds, with a Merkle path that (a) gives a root by the fold of the transaction's own path, which takes exactly the path's tree height of steps, or none for a one-transaction block; (b) names a height at or below the header source's tip at which the header source holds a header; (c) gives a root equal to the Merkle root of that header (bytes 36 to 67 of the 80-byte header, in internal byte order); (d) is for a transaction that is not exactly 64 bytes long; and (e) at no step of the own path hashes 64 bytes, the two nodes in the order hashed, that decode as a transaction. Any field carried with the material that asserts confirmation, a height or a header included, is a claim, and is not evidence of an anchor.
- **Decodes as a transaction**, for 64 bytes: read in the standard transaction serialization, they give, ending exactly at byte 64, a 4-byte version; an input count of at least 1; each input as a 32-byte txid, a 4-byte index, a script length and that many bytes, and a 4-byte sequence number; an output count of at least 1; each output as an 8-byte value, a script length and that many bytes, with every value, and the running sum of the values, at most 2,100,000,000,000,000; and a 4-byte lock time. Counts and lengths are VarInts, in any of their forms.
- **Unconfirmed set U**: the transactions the walk of section 2.1 reaches that are not anchors. The payment is in U unless it is itself an anchor.
- **Unresolved input**: an input of a transaction in U whose source transaction the maker does not hold.

### 2. Making a statement

#### 2.1 The walk

The maker starts at the payment and visits each transaction once, in whatever order the material holds them. It reads the Merkle path of each visited transaction that has one, as BRC-74 permits (below). A path that meets condition (b) of an anchor is checked as one (section 1), and its transaction is not followed further. A path that does not meet condition (b), because it names a height above the tip or one for which the header source holds no header, is not checked: the maker treats its transaction as if it carried no path. Every other visited transaction joins U, and each of its inputs is followed to the transaction it spends, or recorded as unresolved if the maker does not hold that transaction. The inputs come from the held bytes, never from anything carried beside them. The maker decodes only the transactions the walk visits. It reads the rest of the material only as far as its framing needs and, in an Atomic BEEF, as far as each entry's txid and the outpoints its inputs name, to tell an ancestor from an unrelated entry. Framing a BEEF reads every path in it, and a node's flag sets the node's length, so a path that holds a flag other than `00`, `01` or `02` cannot be framed: it is `MALFORMED` whether or not a visited transaction names it.

A BEEF V2 entry that carries only a txid holds no transaction, so an input that spends it is unresolved. [BRC-96](./0096.md) treats such an entry as valid by agreement between the parties exchanging the BEEF, and that agreement is not evidence a statement records. A maker that holds the transaction MAY make its statement from its records instead.

An Atomic BEEF that lacks an ancestor is material here. [BRC-95](./0095.md) fails the validation of such an Atomic BEEF; a maker reads it, and the statement names the inputs that are unresolved. A statement about it does not say that it passes BRC-95 validation. BRC-95's other rules apply as written: the subject, the 32 bytes after the prefix read in internal byte order, is the payment, and the material holds the subject and its ancestors and nothing else. An ancestor is any entry the material holds whose txid an input of the subject, or of another ancestor, names, an anchor's inputs included. A transaction beyond an anchor is therefore an ancestor: BRC-95 permits it, the byte bound counts it, and the walk does not visit it. An entry that carries only a txid has no inputs, so it names no further ancestor, and one whose txid no such input names is unrelated, as an unrelated transaction is.

A maker MUST refuse, with the code given, and return no statement:

| Code | When |
|---|---|
| `MALFORMED` | Material that does not decode completely: bytes missing or left over (a decoder is told its input is complete). A BEEF whose version is not `0100beef` or `0200beef`, so the legacy `efbe0001` too. An Atomic BEEF whose subject is not the payment, or that holds an entry other than the subject and its ancestors, an entry that carries only a txid included. A BEEF that holds one txid twice. A path in a BEEF that holds a flag other than `00`, `01` or `02`, whether or not a visited transaction names it. A payment not in the material. Bytes whose txid differs from the txid they are held under. A transaction in U with no inputs or no outputs. An input naming an output that its held source does not have. A path of a visited transaction that BRC-74 does not permit (below). |
| `ANCHOR_MISMATCH` | A Merkle path that meets condition (b) of an anchor fails condition (a), (c), (d) or (e). The transaction is not treated as unconfirmed instead. |
| `CONFLICT` | Two inputs of transactions in U spend the same output, within one transaction or across two; or a transaction in U spends an output that an anchor the walk reached also spends. |
| `OVER_BOUND` | The maker reads more bytes than its bound: for a BEEF, its whole length, with any Atomic prefix, since the maker frames all of it; for records, the bytes of each transaction the walk visits and of the path held for each of those, as BRC-74 bytes (an Electrum-form branch as converted), and nothing else the records hold. Or the walk reads more transactions, anchors and U together, than its bound; or the longest path through U holds more transactions than its bound on distance. Or the statement, in its canonical form (section 4), is longer than the maker's bound on statement length. |

A path that BRC-74 does not permit is one with a tree height of 0 or more than 64; a flag other than `00`, `01` or `02`; two nodes at one offset of a level; a node outside the tree, at an offset of `2^(h - i)` or more at level `i` of a tree of height `h`; or a node, given as a hash or as the duplicate of its sibling, that differs from the node computed from the two nodes below it. These are checked on the path of every visited transaction, whether or not it meets condition (b), and the flags on every path of a BEEF, since framing reads them. Nodes off a transaction's own path take part in these checks and in nothing else.

A maker MUST have all four bounds and MUST refuse beyond them, never describe part of a graph. It MUST NOT emit a statement longer than its bound on statement length, and refuses `OVER_BOUND` instead. Their values are policy. A maker SHOULD use 1,000,000 bytes as its bound on statement length, the value section 5 recommends for a reader's bound, so that a reader with that bound reads every statement a maker with it emits.

After a reorganisation that replaces the block a path names, the header source holds another header at that height, and material that holds the path is refused `ANCHOR_MISMATCH` (condition c) until its path is replaced.

#### 2.2 Anchoring and distance

- `fullyAnchored` is true when no input is unresolved.
- `distanceMax`, when the payment is fully anchored, is the number of transactions of U on the longest path from the payment back to an anchor. It is 1 when every input of the payment spends an anchor's output, and 0 when the payment is itself an anchor. It is null when the payment is not fully anchored.
- `distanceMin` is the number of transactions of U on the shortest path from the payment back to an anchor, whether or not the payment is fully anchored. It is 0 when the payment is itself an anchor, and null when no path reaches an anchor.

A path's number of transactions of U equals its number of edges from the payment to the anchor: for a chain D, C, B, A in which D spends C, C spends B, B spends A and only A is anchored, both distances are 3.

#### 2.3 Validation

A transaction X of U is **validated** when the maker has run each check below on X and on every transaction of U that X descends from, and every check passed. Only a transaction whose ancestry is fully anchored can be validated, because the checks read the outputs it spends.

1. **Scripts.** For each input, the unlocking script followed by the locking script of the output it spends evaluates to true under the consensus rules in force for the block after the header source's tip. Some of those rules depend on the height at which the spent output was created: for an output that an anchor created, the height of the anchor's block, and for an output that a transaction of U created, the block after the tip, as SV Node judges an output created in its mempool ([`validation.cpp` L2667-L2674](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/validation.cpp#L2667-L2674) and [L2692-L2696](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/validation.cpp#L2692-L2696)). On mainnet the Genesis upgrade activated at height 620,538 and the Chronicle upgrade at 943,816 ([SV Node `chainparams.cpp` L18-L23](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/chainparams.cpp#L18-L23)); for example, `OP_2MUL` is enabled for an output created at or above 943,816 and disabled for one created below it ([`interpreter.cpp` L360-L372](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/script/interpreter.cpp#L360-L372)). A maker whose next block is below an upgrade's activation height applies the rules from before it. This check includes every signature check ([BRC-67](./0067.md) step 1).
2. **Amounts.** Each output's value, and the sum of X's outputs, is from 0 to 2,100,000,000,000,000 satoshis, and the outputs X spends add up to at least the sum of its outputs. [BRC-67](./0067.md) step 2 asks for more than the outputs, as a stand-in for a fee rate. This check asks only that no value is created, and section 2.4 reports the fee.
3. **Lock time.** X can be mined in the block after the header source's tip as it stands: its `nLockTime` is 0; or every input's sequence number is `0xFFFFFFFF`; or its `nLockTime` is below 500,000,000 and below that block's height; or its `nLockTime` is 500,000,000 or more and below the median time past (section 1). An input that spends an output of an anchored coinbase transaction does so only if that block's height is at least the coinbase's height plus 100, the network's coinbase maturity ([SV Node `consensus.h` L75](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/consensus/consensus.h#L75)). An input that spends an output of an anchored transaction whose first output's locking script begins `006a0463667478` (`OP_FALSE OP_RETURN` and a push of `cftx`), a confiscation transaction ([`frozentxo_db.cpp` L614-L636](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/frozentxo_db.cpp#L614-L636)), does so only if that block's height is at least the anchor's height plus 1,000 ([`consensus.h` L79](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/consensus/consensus.h#L79), [`validation.cpp` L2627-L2636](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/validation.cpp#L2627-L2636)). Before Genesis, the relative lock times of BIP 68, set by each input's sequence number, also bound a transaction of version 2 or above ([`validation.cpp` L272-L273](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/validation.cpp#L272-L273) and [L3463-L3468](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/validation.cpp#L3463-L3468), [`policy.h` L247-L255](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/policy/policy.h#L247-L255)); a maker whose next block is below the Genesis activation height applies them too. [BRC-67](./0067.md) step 4 asks that `nLockTime` and each `nSequence` be set to the values the receiver expects. This check asks only whether X can be mined in the next block; which values a receiver expects is its policy.

Only a check that ran under the consensus rules and failed is a refusal, and the maker then returns no statement:

| Code | When |
|---|---|
| `SCRIPT_FAILED` | Check 1 ran, and an evaluation ended false or broke a consensus rule. |
| `VALUE_CREATED` | Check 2 fails. |
| `PREMATURE` | Check 3 fails: X cannot be mined in the next block as it stands. This does not find X invalid. A transaction whose lock time is still to come may be meant to be replaced before then, as in the payment channels of [BRC-9](./0009.md) step 9, and a statement can be made once a later tip lets check 3 pass. |

A maker MUST NOT refuse because of a limit it sets for itself, such as a bound on a script's memory, operations or number length, or because of a stricter rule it applies for its own use. When the maker cannot complete a check on a transaction, it MUST leave that transaction, and every transaction of U that descends from it, out of the validated set, and MUST NOT refuse because of it. It cannot complete a check when its interpreter does not implement an opcode the script uses under the rules in force, or treats it otherwise than those rules do (as disabled, as invalid or as doing nothing); when an evaluation reaches one of its own limits; when a lock time needs a median time past it does not have; or when check 3 needs a relative lock time and the maker does not implement those. Nor can it complete the checks on a transaction of U whose own first output's locking script begins `006a0463667478`: that is a confiscation transaction, which SV Node accepts only when it is whitelisted ([`validation.cpp` L2562-L2587](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/validation.cpp#L2562-L2587)), and a maker cannot know the whitelist. A maker MAY also leave out a transaction that passes these checks but not a stricter check of its own, and it need not check a transaction that descends from one it left out. A statement records only checks that passed: a maker MUST NOT count as validated a transaction whose checks were not all completed.

A maker that ran a check and found that it failed MUST refuse. It MUST NOT then report that transaction as not checked, or make a distance-only statement about the same payment from material that holds it, while the failure stands; for `PREMATURE`, that is until a later tip lets check 3 pass.

`validated` lists the validated transactions. `validation` is `"all"` when the payment is fully anchored and every transaction of U is validated, `"none"` when U is not empty and no transaction is validated, and `"partial"` otherwise. A maker that runs no checks reports `"none"`, and conforms. A payment that is itself an anchor has an empty U, so its `validation` is `"all"` whether or not the maker validates: the checks of this section apply to U, so both kinds of maker have run the same checks on such a payment, and both make the same statement.

When more than one refusal applies, a maker MAY report any of them.

#### 2.4 Fee

`lowestFeeRate` covers validated transactions only. It is the fee and size of the validated transaction whose fee per byte is lowest, as `{"bytes": size, "satoshis": fee}`, where the fee is what its inputs spend less what its outputs pay, and the size is the length of its bytes. Among equal rates it is the one with the smallest txid. It is null when no transaction is validated. Two integers carry the rate without rounding. Whether the rate is enough for a miner is policy.

#### 2.5 Status and observations

`status` is `{"depth": d, "event": e}`, where `e` is the event furthest down this table among those the maker observed for the payment, whatever order it observed them in:

| `event` | Meaning | `depth` |
|---|---|---|
| `received` | The maker holds the payment, from another party. | null |
| `signed-locally` | The maker signed the payment itself. | null |
| `handed-to-broadcaster` | The maker gave the payment to a broadcaster. | null |
| `seen-by-network` | A node or service reported holding the payment among its unmined transactions. | null |
| `mined` | The payment is itself an anchor. | `tip.height` less the anchor's height, plus 1 |

A maker MUST say `mined` when the payment is itself an anchor, and MUST NOT say it otherwise, whatever it was told.

`conflicts` is `{"checked": false}`, or a check the maker made for a conflicting spend:

```
{"at": t, "checked": true, "found": f, "kind": k, "scope": s}
```

- `at`: when the observation was made, in Unix seconds, no later than `madeAt`.
- `found`: true if a transaction other than those in U was observed spending an output in scope.
- `kind`: `own-node`, a node the maker operates, or `third-party`, a node or service operated by another party.
- `scope`: `payment`, the outputs the payment's own inputs spend, or `unconfirmed`, the outputs spent by every transaction of U.

How the observation is made is operation, and outside this document. `found: false` says only that none was seen at time `at`.

`madeAt` is when the statement was made, in Unix seconds.

`tip` is `{"blockHash": b, "height": n}`: the header source's tip when the statement was made, and the double SHA-256 of its header in display order. Every depth and lock-time verdict in the statement is for that tip: check 3 was made for the block at `n + 1`.

### 3. The statement

A statement is a JSON object with exactly these fields:

| Field | Type | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `anchors` | array | One `{"blockHash", "height", "txid"}` per anchor the walk reached, sorted by `txid`. `blockHash` is the double SHA-256 of the header the path reached, in display order. |
| `conflicts` | object | Section 2.5. |
| `distanceMax` | integer or null | Section 2.2. |
| `distanceMin` | integer or null | Section 2.2. |
| `fullyAnchored` | boolean | Section 2.2. |
| `lowestFeeRate` | object or null | Section 2.4. |
| `madeAt` | integer | Section 2.5. |
| `status` | object | Section 2.5. |
| `tip` | object | Section 2.5. |
| `txid` | string | The payment. |
| `unconfirmed` | array | The txids of U, sorted. |
| `unresolved` | array | One `{"txid", "vout"}` per unresolved input, the output it spends, sorted by `txid` and then by `vout`. |
| `validated` | array | The txids of the validated transactions, sorted. |
| `validation` | string | `none`, `partial` or `all` (section 2.3). |
| `version` | string | `bsv-payment-evidence/v1` |

Every txid and block hash is 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits, in display order. Every array is in strictly ascending order of the strings and integers named, so no txid (or txid and vout) is in it twice.

### 4. Canonical form

A statement is exchanged as the bytes of its canonical form:

- keys sorted by their bytes at every level, with no whitespace and the separators `,` and `:`;
- integers in decimal, from 0 to 2^53 - 1, with no sign, leading zero, fraction or exponent; there are no other numbers;
- strings only from the values of section 3, so no character is ever escaped; `null`, `true` and `false` as JSON writes them;
- the whole encoded as ASCII.

This is the canonical form of [BRC-179](../wallet/0179.md) section 7.2, extended with integers and `null` as above. A maker MUST emit it.

### 5. Reading a statement

A reader has a bound on the length of the bytes it reads. Its value is policy, and a reader SHOULD use 1,000,000 bytes, the value section 2.1 recommends for a maker's bound on statement length. A reader MUST, in this order:

1. refuse `OVER_BOUND` if the bytes are longer than its bound, before it parses them;
2. refuse `MALFORMED_STATEMENT` unless the bytes are ASCII, nest arrays and objects at most 32 deep (the outermost object is depth 1), and parse as a JSON object, with no key twice in one object, whose `version` is a string;
3. refuse `UNKNOWN_VERSION` if `version` is not `bsv-payment-evidence/v1`, and read no further;
4. refuse `MALFORMED_STATEMENT` if the bytes are not the canonical form of the object they parse to; if a field is missing, unexpected or of the wrong type (a boolean is `true` or `false`, never a number); if a txid or block hash is not 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits; if a `vout` is above 2^32 - 1, the largest output index a transaction can name; if an array is not in the strictly ascending order section 3 gives; if `validation`, `status.event`, `conflicts.kind` or `conflicts.scope` has a value that sections 2.3 and 2.5 do not list; or if the fields disagree with one another:
   - `fullyAnchored` is true exactly when `unresolved` is empty;
   - no txid is in both `anchors` and `unconfirmed`;
   - the payment is either in `anchors`, with `unconfirmed` empty, or in `unconfirmed`;
   - `distanceMax` is null exactly when `fullyAnchored` is false, and `distanceMin` is null exactly when `anchors` is empty;
   - for a payment in `anchors`, `anchors` holds that entry only and both distances are 0; otherwise a fully anchored payment has an anchor, a distance that is not null is at least 1 and at most the length of `unconfirmed`, and `distanceMin` is at most `distanceMax`;
   - `validated` is within `unconfirmed`, and `validation` is the value section 2.3 gives for these lists;
   - the payment is in `validated` only if `validated` equals `unconfirmed` and `fullyAnchored` is true;
   - the txid of an `unresolved` entry is not in `anchors`, `unconfirmed` or `validated`;
   - `lowestFeeRate` is null exactly when `validated` is empty, and its `bytes` is at least 1;
   - `status.event` is `mined` exactly when the payment is in `anchors`, and `status.depth` is a positive integer then and null otherwise;
   - `conflicts.at`, when present, is at most `madeAt`;
   - every anchor's `height` is at most `tip.height`, and for a payment in `anchors`, `status.depth` is `tip.height` less the payment's anchor height, plus 1.

A reader MUST NOT take a statement to say more than its fields do. A fully anchored payment is not a mined payment. Validation `all` does not mean the payment will be mined. `found: false` does not mean that no conflicting spend exists.

### 6. Merkle branches in the Electrum form

The Electrum protocol's `blockchain.transaction.get_merkle` returns a Merkle branch as `{"block_height": h, "merkle": [...], "pos": p}`: the sibling hashes from the bottom of the tree up, each as hex in display byte order, and the transaction's index in the block. Electrum-protocol servers return branches in this form. A maker MAY hold paths in this form, and raw transactions held with such branches are material (section 1). A maker that converts a branch to a [BRC-74](./0074.md) path MUST:

1. compute the txid from the raw transaction it holds;
2. refuse `MALFORMED`, and make no path, if the raw transaction does not decode completely; if the branch is not an object of exactly `block_height`, `merkle` and `pos`, with non-negative integers `h` and `p` (`h` below 2^64, since BRC-74 writes it as a VarInt) and each sibling 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits; if it has more than 64 siblings (BRC-74's limit on a tree's height); or if `p` is `2^n` or more, where `n` is the number of siblings (with no sibling, `p` is 0). The conversion refuses nothing else;
3. write the path with block height `h` and tree height `n`, or, with no sibling, tree height 1 and the transaction alone at offset 0, a one-transaction block. Level 0 holds the transaction at offset `p` with flag `02` and its txid in internal byte order, and, when `n > 0`, the first sibling at offset `p XOR 1`. Each level `i` from 1 to `n - 1` holds sibling `i` at offset `(p >> i) XOR 1`. Nodes in a level are in ascending offset. A sibling equal to the working hash at a level where the working node's offset is even is written with flag `01` and no hash; every other sibling is written with flag `00` and its hash in internal byte order.

The conversion hashes the branch only to find those duplicates. The converted path is a Merkle path like any other: it anchors only through the checks of section 1, against the maker's own header source, so a step that hashes 64 bytes that decode as a transaction is refused `ANCHOR_MISMATCH` there, when the path is checked. Only the siblings, `h` and `p` are used. A maker that holds a branch and does not convert it MUST check it as if converted, with the same refusals and the same anchor. Folding the branch as the Electrum protocol does and folding the converted path give the same root.

### 7. Signing (optional)

A maker MAY sign the canonical bytes of a statement as a [BRC-77](../peer-to-peer/0077.md) message. The BRC-77 serialization travels beside the statement, never inside it. A verifier checks it over the exact bytes it received; any other serialization of the same object does not verify. A signature says who made a statement and adds nothing to what the statement claims.

### 8. Offering statements to applications (optional)

A wallet that implements [BRC-100](../wallet/0100.md) MAY offer one additional method, `getPaymentEvidence`:

- **Arguments.** Either `txid`, a payment the wallet holds, or `atomicBEEF`, an Atomic BEEF ([BRC-95](./0095.md)) for the wallet to evaluate without storing it; and `sign`, a boolean, false when omitted. For `atomicBEEF` the material is that Atomic BEEF alone: the wallet's records resolve none of its inputs, so any party with the same bytes and the same headers can make the same statement. For `atomicBEEF`, too, `status.event` is `received` unless the payment is an anchor, whatever the wallet's records say of the payment, and `conflicts` is `{"checked":false}` or an observation made for this call.
- **Result.** `statement`, the statement as a string, and, when `sign` is true, `signature`, the BRC-77 serialization as hex. A string keeps the bytes exact across transports that re-serialize objects.
- **Errors.** A refusal is a BRC-100 error, with `status` `"error"`, a `code` of `ERR_EVIDENCE_` followed by the refusal code of section 2 (for example `ERR_EVIDENCE_ANCHOR_MISMATCH`), and a `description`; no statement is returned. For `txid`, a payment the wallet does not hold and a call the user has not permitted for that payment both give the code `ERR_EVIDENCE_UNKNOWN_TXID` with the same `description`. A call with neither `txid` nor `atomicBEEF` gives `ERR_MISSING_PARAMETER`, one with both gives `ERR_CONFLICTING_PARAMETERS`, and a `sign` that is not a boolean gives `ERR_INVALID_PARAMETER_VALUE`, the codes BRC-100 suggests for those cases; none returns a statement.

A wallet that offers the method MUST return the statement's canonical bytes (section 4) unchanged, and its errors as above.

The method is not one of BRC-100's methods. It changes no BRC-100 argument, result, call code or default behaviour, and an application that does not call it sees no change. This document assigns it no wire call code. As BRC-100's compatibility contract requires of an unsupported extension, a wallet that does not offer the method fails the call explicitly, and that failure says nothing about the payment.

An application learns whether a wallet offers the method by calling it, never from the `getVersion` string ([BRC-205](../wallet/0205.md) section 6), and a wallet that publishes a [BRC-179](../wallet/0179.md) capability manifest declares it there as `<product>.<surface>.getPaymentEvidence`, at the tier of its most sensitive step.

## Rationale

**Two distances.** The longest path is the worst case. A payment is good only if every input is good, and a short path through one input says nothing about a long path through another, so a limit on unconfirmed depth compares with `distanceMax`. `distanceMin`, the distance to the nearest anchor, exists even when some input is unresolved. On a chain the two are equal; on S5 they are 3 and 1. Carrying both lets an application see the gap.

**A failed check is a refusal.** A statement that recorded a failed check would rely on every application reading it correctly in order not to accept an invalid payment. A refusal cannot be misread, and it keeps `none` meaning "not checked".

**Not checked is not failed.** A validation refusal (section 2.3) says the payment cannot be mined as it stands, so it is kept for checks that ran under the consensus rules and failed. A maker's own limits, and rules its interpreter does not implement, only leave transactions out of the validated set: the statement then says less, and nothing false.

**A path that does not match is a refusal.** A Merkle path that leads to a root other than that of the header the maker trusts at its height is contradicted evidence: it is fabricated, or from a block the maker's chain does not hold. Treating its transaction as unconfirmed would hide that. A path above the tip, or at a height for which the header source holds no header, is neither confirmed nor contradicted, so its transaction is walked as an unconfirmed one, and a later statement may anchor it. A path that BRC-74 does not permit is wrong whatever the headers say, so it is refused before any header is read.

**The own path.** Two inner nodes read as a transaction are 64 bytes long, so condition (d), which never anchors a 64-byte transaction, keeps inner nodes from standing for a transaction. Condition (e) keeps a transaction from standing for inner nodes, and, like condition (a), it concerns only the steps that fold the transaction's own txid, since only those decide what the path proves about that transaction. Nodes elsewhere in a compound path are checked against one another and nothing more: a pair among them that decodes as a transaction proves nothing about this transaction either way (vector S14).

**Partial material is reported.** A statement exists to say what is missing, so an Atomic BEEF that lacks an ancestor is read and the gap is named. Refusing it would leave a receiver that holds partial evidence no way to say so. Bytes that are wrong in themselves, such as an unrelated entry in an Atomic BEEF, a txid twice or a path BRC-74 does not permit, are refused.

**Bounds refuse.** A walk cut short would report unresolved inputs for a reason a reader cannot see. A statement longer than a reader's bound is refused unread, so a maker refuses to emit one longer than its own, and one recommended value for both keeps a maker and a reader that use it in agreement.

**Status apart from evidence.** Broadcast, relay and mining are events the maker observes, and the evidence fields are checks it ran. Keeping them apart stops a report of the first kind standing in for the second. `mined` is the one status the evidence itself supports, so it is the one status derived from it. The tip is named so that a reader can check the depth, and the lock-time verdict, against its own headers.

## Implementations

A reference implementation exists: a module of a wallet that makes a statement from an Atomic BEEF or from the wallet's records, in either mode, converts Electrum-form branches, and reads statements. Its script checks run on the interpreter of the wallet's existing BEEF verifier, the one its incoming payments pass, and it counts an input as passed only when that verifier accepts it. That interpreter predates Chronicle, so the implementation leaves out, and never refuses, a transaction whose script uses an opcode Chronicle restored or a signature with the sighash bit `0x20`, or a transaction of version 2 or above whose script fails there. It does not implement the relative lock times of section 2.3, check 3, either, and leaves out a transaction that needs one.

## Security Considerations

**A statement is not a guarantee.** A fully anchored payment with validation `all` has valid spends from proven coins to the payment. It can still go unmined: a conflicting spend may reach miners first, or its fee may be too low for them. A statement records evidence; the receiver's own policy decides whether to accept the payment.

**Who made it.** A statement says what its maker checked. An unsigned statement from the payer, or from any party with an interest in the payment being accepted, is no evidence: relying on it is relying on that party's word. A receiver makes its own statement from the material, or reads one from a maker it trusts, signed (section 7) when it has travelled.

**The header source.** An anchor is only as good as the header that confirms it. A source that serves headers whose proof of work nobody checked lets whoever controls it turn any Merkle path into an anchor. A maker SHOULD use headers whose proof of work it has checked. A reader of another party's statement can check the tip and each anchor's height and block hash against its own headers.

**A 64-byte transaction.** A block header does not say how tall the block's Merkle tree is, and the txid of a 64-byte transaction is computed as an inner node of the tree is, the double SHA-256 of 64 bytes. That allows two attacks, and the anchor definition closes each. In the first, two sibling inner nodes, read together as a transaction, have a Merkle path to the real root: whoever orders a block's transactions can search for an order in which such a pair also parses as a transaction with an output anyone can spend. Condition (d) closes it, since a 64-byte transaction is never an anchor. In the second, a real 64-byte transaction is mined, and its two halves are offered as the bottom step of a path one level taller, so that a transaction whose txid is the first half appears proven; vector M19 builds that shape, a txid with `01` at byte 4 beside a sibling that completes a transaction. Condition (e) closes it, since no step of the own path may hash 64 bytes that decode as a transaction. Neither condition alone is enough. No ordinary payment needs a 64-byte transaction, which has 4 bytes for all of its scripts together. A miner who orders a block can place beside an honest transaction a sibling it searched for, so that the transaction's path fails (e); that payment is then refused, never wrongly accepted.

**Bounds.** A payment can have very many ancestors, and every script costs time to evaluate. A maker bounds the material's size in bytes and the walk before it does that work (section 2.1), and a maker that evaluates scripts for others SHOULD also limit the work each script may do. Reaching such a limit leaves a transaction unvalidated; it is never a refusal (section 2.3). A statement grows with the payment, since each unconfirmed transaction and each unresolved input adds an entry: one transaction of hundreds of thousands of inputs whose sources are not held gives a statement of tens of megabytes. A maker bounds the statement it emits, and a reader the bytes it parses, at 1,000,000 bytes by default for both (sections 2.1 and 5).

**Frozen outputs.** SV Node refuses a transaction that spends an output on its freeze lists ([`validation.cpp` L2596-L2607](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/6504a3aff65ba97c0f6c80962b033e35ecbfed4b/src/validation.cpp#L2596-L2607)). No header carries those lists, so the checks of section 2.3 do not cover them: a validated transaction can spend a frozen output and never be mined.

**Observations age.** `found: false` held at time `at`, a status held at `madeAt`, and the depth at `tip`. A later conflicting spend or a later block changes none of those fields.

**The method as an oracle.** An application that may call the method of section 8 with any txid could learn which transactions the wallet holds, if the wallet answered calls it has not permitted otherwise than calls about a transaction it does not hold. Section 8 gives both the same error. With `atomicBEEF`, the status is `received` unless the payment is an anchor, and a conflict check is one made for that call, so the answer depends on the bytes, the wallet's headers and that check, and on nothing the wallet's records hold.

**Privacy.** A statement lists txids of the payment's ancestry, and a signed one links the maker's key to the payment. A maker SHOULD give a statement only to whom the user chose, such as the application that asked about that payment.

- No conflicting-spend observation was made: the vectors record the fields only.
- The bounds on statement length are exercised at one value each: the reader's in R18 and the maker's in M25.
- No wallet that reports ancestry distance today, BREAD in ElectrumSVP included, has produced a statement.
